Three steps to help your household get started.

Geological hazards originate from processes within the Earth or movements of its surface. They include earthquakes, landslides and ground subsidence. Understanding faults and unstable slopes helps explain how these hazards can threaten settlements and roads. Heavy rainfall can also trigger landslides, showing how natural processes interact.

Hydrological hazards involve the movement and distribution of water, including river flooding. Water can damage homes, farmland and transport routes. The effects vary between places because exposure, building conditions, terrain, drainage and access to support are different.

UNDERSTANDING MYANMAR

Disasters affect more than the landscape.

Communities in Myanmar experience disruption from earthquakes, flooding and other natural hazards. Understanding a disaster means looking beyond the event itself to the people, homes and everyday activities affected.

These documented examples connect reported figures with their meaning for communities. Each set of statistics belongs to the date and geographical area stated below.

Earthquake impacts

01 / HUMAN IMPACT

The 2025 earthquake

Earthquakes struck central Myanmar on 28 March 2025. UNICEF's subsequent update described continuing humanitarian needs across affected communities.

The figures below are the official deaths and injuries reported as of 7 May, quoted by UNICEF. They are a dated snapshot, rather than a final total.

Reported deaths
3,791
Reported injuries
5,106

Figures as of 7 May 2025. Report published 9 May 2025.

UNICEF — Earthquake Flash Update No. 12
Flood impacts

02 / WIDESPREAD FLOODING

The September 2024 floods

Monsoon rain and the remnants of Typhoon Yagi caused widespread flooding. OCHA estimated that one million people in 70 townships were affected by 26 September.

Its 27 September report recorded more than 360 reported drowning deaths. People affected and deaths describe different impacts; they must not be treated as interchangeable figures.

People affected, estimated
1 million
Reported drowning deaths
360+

Affected population as of 26 September 2024. Deaths reported in the 27 September update.

UN OCHA — Flood Situation Report No. 3
Homes and schools

03 / DAMAGE TO DAILY LIFE

Rebuilding homes and schools

UNICEF reported extensive damage to homes and schools after the March earthquake. Many displaced families remained in temporary shelters as heat and early rains worsened conditions.

Rebuilding was limited by scarce, unaffordable materials. School damage disrupted learning, while some school buildings were also being used as temporary accommodation.

Homes damaged or destroyed
55,000+
Schools damaged or destroyed
2,500+

Reported by UNICEF on 9 May 2025. These figures include both damage and destruction.

UNICEF — Earthquake Flash Update No. 12
Livelihood impacts

04 / LIVELIHOODS AND RECOVERY

When floods affect household income

Flooding damaged farmland and livestock, affecting the resources families depended on for their livelihoods.

OCHA reported nearly 48,500 acres of crops submerged in southern and eastern Shan, and more than 2,600 livestock lost across eastern Bago, Kayin and Mon. These figures cover different areas, not all of Myanmar.

Reported on 27 September 2024. Acres submerged do not necessarily mean crops destroyed.
