PUBLISHED RESEARCH & EVIDENCE

What real disasters in Myanmar can teach us.

Our project uses secondary research: information already published by organisations such as the United Nations, humanitarian agencies, scientific institutions and Myanmar mapping services.

These reports document the scale of major disasters and show why evacuation planning, early warnings, safe shelter and community preparedness matter.

How to read the figures

Each number belongs to a specific event and reporting date. Estimates can change as new information becomes available.

Event Reported figure What the figure describes Original source

Figures refer to different events and measure different things. They should not be added together or treated as directly comparable. These are historical reports, not current emergency alerts.

ANALYSING THE EVIDENCE

What can we learn from these events?

These observations connect published disaster reports with practical preparedness. They are interpretations of the evidence rather than predictions.

01

Coastal communities need evacuation plans.

Cyclone Nargis caused severe destruction in the Ayeyarwady Delta and Yangon Region. Its impacts demonstrate the danger of powerful winds, storm surges and coastal flooding. Communities in exposed areas benefit from accessible evacuation information, safer shelter and communication plans.

Read the UN report
02

Flooding affects much more than buildings.

Reports on the September 2024 floods described damage to farmland, roads, bridges and essential services. Flood preparedness therefore includes more than protecting a house: people also need plans for drinking water, transport, medication and temporary accommodation.

Read the flood situation report
03

Earthquake preparedness must consider infrastructure.

The March 2025 earthquake damaged homes, schools, hospitals and other important facilities. Preparedness should therefore include safe actions during shaking, household emergency supplies, communication arrangements and awareness of the risks from damaged buildings and disrupted services.

Read the earthquake snapshot

OUR RESEARCH APPROACH

How we use published information.

Good research means looking beyond a headline or a single number. It requires checking sources, understanding how figures were measured and explaining the limitations of the available evidence.

01

Select relevant sources

Prioritise official scientific information, UN reports, humanitarian situation updates and established Myanmar mapping resources.

02

Record dates and definitions

Identify when the disaster occurred, when the report was published and whether a figure measures affected people, displacement, deaths or another impact.

03

Compare carefully

Affected population estimates from different events may use different methods. We avoid combining figures that represent different measures.

04

Explain the limitations

Access difficulties, incomplete reporting and changing estimates can affect disaster statistics. We provide links so readers can examine the original evidence.

ABOUT OUR INFORMATION

Useful things to know.

Check who published the information, when it was updated, what evidence it uses and which location it describes. For important claims, compare several trustworthy sources and look for original reports rather than unsupported statements.

Secondary research uses information that has already been collected and published by others, such as scientific studies, government documents, maps and humanitarian reports. Readers should be able to identify the original source and reporting date.

Early reports may contain estimates because communication, transport and access to affected locations are difficult. Later assessments may change the figures. Organisations may also use different definitions for people affected, displaced or in need of assistance.
